# Empty and humane are not the same thing

You've felt this. The calendar shows a green slot at 4 p.m. your time, everyone's free, you book it. Then three minutes before the call you do the math you should have done first: 4 p.m. for you is 7 a.m. for the colleague in another timezone, and they're joining from a kitchen with a kid on their hip. The slot was free. It was not humane.

Most schedulers solve the wrong problem. They find a time the calendars don't collide and call it done. But a gap on someone's calendar at 7 a.m. isn't free time — it's time they're not working, which is a different thing. Overlap is built on that distinction. It doesn't look for a slot nobody booked. It looks for a slot inside everyone's actual working day.

Here's how it works.

## First, people set their real hours — including the edges

Most "working hours" settings are a 9-to-5 guess nobody updates. Overlap asks for something more honest: the edges. The school run at 8:15. The hour you refuse to take calls. The dinner you're not moving. You set this once, and it's the hard boundary, not a suggestion.

> Overlap stores the edges of your day, not just a tidy 9-to-5 that was never true.

The edges are the whole point. A free-slot finder treats your calendar as empty space to fill. Overlap treats your day as having a shape — a start, a stop, and a few lines you've drawn inside it that a meeting shouldn't cross.

## Then, a proposed time gets mapped against everyone at once

When someone proposes a meeting, Overlap lays every attendee's working hours onto one timeline and shows where the meeting actually lands. Not "is this slot empty" — "is this slot inside everyone's working day." Windows that fit for everyone show up clearly. Windows that only work because one person would have to give up an edge get flagged, by name, before you send the invite.

> The bad news arrives while you can still fix it, not three minutes before the call.

This is the part that changes the job. The person who used to catch the timezone problem last — usually the ops lead, usually too late — doesn't have to be the one who notices anymore. The math stops being a human responsibility.

## Last, you pick from the humane windows — or hear the truth

You choose from the windows that fit everyone. Often there are several. Sometimes, with a wide enough spread, there are none — a team across nine timezones doesn't always have a shared waking hour, and pretending otherwise is how someone ends up on a 6 a.m. call every week.

When that happens, Overlap says so plainly. It doesn't bury the conflict in a green checkmark. Instead it suggests an async alternative, or a rotating-inconvenience option so the same person isn't always the one who pays. It will not auto-book, it will not invent a good time that doesn't exist, and it doesn't track anyone's productivity along the way.

> Overlap will tell you when there's no good time. That honesty is the feature.

That's the trade. You give up the comfortable fiction that a perfect slot is always hiding somewhere. You get a tool that finds the real one when it exists and refuses to fake one when it doesn't.
