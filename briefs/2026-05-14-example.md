# Brief — 2026-05-14-focusblock-launch

The first worked example for this repo. Paired with the rendered output in `examples.md`. Deliberately industry-neutral so the example illustrates the architecture without bleeding into any specific operator's domain.

---

## 1. What to produce

One 600-800 word product-launch post for the FocusBlock company blog. FocusBlock is a focus-timer and deep-work scheduler for remote knowledge workers. The post announces v1.0 (general availability), with the angle that most productivity tools optimize for the wrong layer.

## 2. Audience

Remote knowledge workers — engineers, designers, writers, analysts — who manage their own time and have tried at least two productivity systems that didn't stick. They're skeptical of habit-tracker apps and gamification. They already know what a Pomodoro is; they don't need it explained. They read newsletters like *Stratechery* or *Lenny's*, not productivity-influencer Twitter.

## 3. Format and constraints

- 600-800 words.
- Markdown.
- One H1 (the post title).
- Two or three H2 section headings.
- One pull-quote-able sentence per H2 (something the reader could screenshot).
- Ends with a single, specific call to action — a link to the product, not a vague "learn more."
- No bullet lists longer than four items. Long bullets are fine; long lists are not.

## 4. Voice override (or default)

Use the default voice in `reference/voice.md`.

## 5. Success criteria

- Word count is between 600 and 800.
- Reads aloud cleanly.
- Zero buzzwords from the banned list in `reference/voice.md` ("leverage," "synergy," "scalable," "robust," "ecosystem," "thought leader," "10x," "best-in-class," "unlock the power of," "in today's fast-paced world," "let's dive in").
- The "wrong layer" angle is concrete by the third paragraph — names what the wrong layer is and what FocusBlock optimizes for instead.
- The product is mentioned by name at least three times, but not in every paragraph.
- The post does not promise outcomes FocusBlock can't deliver (no "double your output," no testimonials, no statistics).
- Ends with a specific CTA (download link or signup link, not "stay tuned").

## 6. Refusal context

N/A — no third-party voice, no high-harm domain, no claims requiring external sources. All product facts in the brief are simulated for the worked example; in a real dispatch the brief would either ground the product facts itself or reference a `reference/products.md` file.

---

## Product facts (provided by the brief, since this is the example)

- **Product name:** FocusBlock
- **What it is:** A focus-timer + deep-work scheduler. Sits on top of the user's calendar.
- **Key feature:** Blocks 90-minute focus windows on the user's calendar based on the user's actual energy patterns (the app learns from feedback after each session).
- **Differentiator:** Most focus apps optimize for *willpower* — they nag you, gamify you, lock you out of distracting sites. FocusBlock optimizes for *scheduling* — it puts focus on your calendar before willpower has to do any work. By the time the focus window arrives, the decision to focus has already been made (and defended) by the calendar entry.
- **Available:** macOS, Windows, Linux. Free for solo use; $8/month for team features.
- **Launch date:** Today.
- **CTA URL:** `https://focusblock.example/download`

These facts are the only product information the worker has. The post should not invent additional features, outcomes, or testimonials.
